The September 2026 update names three protocols and does not write the scripts in this deck. The follow-on lesson is the step-by-step. Do not invent the words on the air.

2. SALUTE

SALUTE is the observation report: what you saw of an opposing force while on patrol. A good one lets command build a picture of the threat. Send it in SOI codes so the other side does not read it off the air.

This block's letters, from the block's own list:

LetterReport
S — SizeNumber of troops observed.
A — ActivityWhat they are doing. Bivouacked, moving north, repairing equipment, loading vehicles.
L — LocationWhere the element is. A grid goes by shackle, not as raw digits.
U — UniformWhat they are wearing. The pasted reference on the source slide uses "Unit" (which opposing unit) in this slot. This block uses Uniform.
T — TimeWhen you observed them.
E — EquipmentGear: vehicles, weapons, and the rest.
SALT is the abbreviated version of SALUTE. This deck names it and does not say which letters it drops. Do not guess a short form and send that.

3. LACE

LACE is the team's own status, sent to the TOC from the field. It is the whole troop, not one person's comfort.

LetterOn the airWhat it is
L — LiquidLimaUsually water. How much the element still has.
A — AmmoAlphaAmmunition. Combat effectiveness.
C — CasualtiesCharlieHow many casualties the element has.
E — EquipmentEchoEquipment pertinent to the mission. Can you still do the job with what you have.

Each letter is a color. The block calls these levels of progressive desperation:

ColorMeaning
GreenAll good.
YellowNot perfect, but still mission effective.
RedProblems that pose significant issues to the mission or the team's well-being.
BlackThe mission cannot continue under current conditions.

4. LACE on the air

Contact first. Do not start the report until they tell you to send it. Break between letters so no transmission runs past 8 seconds. The block's example:

StationTransmission
Blackfoot 9Geronimo Main, Geronimo Main, this is Blackfoot 9, over
Geronimo MainBlackfoot 9, this is Geronimo Main, over
Blackfoot 9Geronimo Main, this is Blackfoot 9, LACE report incoming, over
Geronimo MainBlackfoot 9, this is Geronimo Main, send it, over
Blackfoot 9Geronimo Main, this is Blackfoot 9, Lima — Green — break. Alpha — Yellow — break. Charlie — Green — break. Echo — Green — over
Geronimo MainBlackfoot 9, this is Geronimo Main, Roger, out
WHY THE BREAKS: The block says the breaks are there to keep each transmission under 8 continuous seconds. Unkey on each break. Geronimo Main closes with Roger, out — receipt, and no answer expected.

5. CaseEvac — 9-line, then MIST

The block calls the casualty evacuation request a TSM modified 9-line. Send it clear and concise. Lines 1 through 5 go on the initial call. Lines 6 through 9 follow while the aircraft is en route. A MIST report comes after the 9-line.

LineSend
1Location of the pickup site. Grid of the extraction point.
2Radio frequency, call sign, and suffix for the pickup site.
3Number of patients by precedence. A urgent. B urgent surgical. C priority. D routine. E convenience.
4Special equipment. A none. B hoist. C extraction equipment. D ventilator.
5Number of patients by type. L litter. A ambulatory. Give the count for each.
6Security of the pickup site. N none. P possible. E enemy. X engaged. Enemy troops, not the patient's condition.
7Method of marking the pickup site. Panels, pyrotechnics, smoke, or another signal.
8Patient nationality and status. A is US military. B is US civilian.
9Wartime: NBC contamination (nuclear, biological, or chemical). Peacetime: terrain at the site.
LINES 1–5 FIRST. Do not hold the aircraft request for lines 6 through 9. Those go out while it is already en route. A grid on line 1 still goes by shackle, not as raw digits.
