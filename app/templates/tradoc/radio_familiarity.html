Radio Familiarity

13th Legion — Training Reference

Comms Basics · Training Block 3 · 2026

1. What this lesson is

This is the radio half of Comms Basics: who is on which net, and how you keep that net from telling on you. How you talk once you have the station is Net Etiquette. What you send once you have them is Reports.

The block is foundational only. It is meant to be applied and practiced on FTXs, not memorized once and shelved.

3. Communication security — always

ComSec applies in the field and out of it. It is more than the radio in your hand.

  • Do not share frequencies, channels, or intel. Need-to-know.
  • Minimize the RF footprint. That is radio settings, where you put the radio, and sound discipline.
  • The 3 C's: Clear, Concise, Correct. A short correct transmission is a smaller footprint than a long one.
  • Callsigns on the radio only. Not in the open, and not as a habit off the net.
  • Codewords, brevity codes, and shackle codes are how you say the thing without saying the thing.

5. Shackle codes

A shackle code is a 10-letter word. Each letter is one digit, 0 through 9. You send the letter, not the number. This block uses shackle for RTB authentication and for relaying a grid.

The training key in this block:

LetterSCUBADIVER
Digit0123456789
HOW YOU USE IT: A 4 is "A". A 0 is "S". A grid that starts 10 starts "C S". Read it as letters on the air. The word SCUBADIVER is the training key on the slide, not a standing live code.

6. When you cannot raise them

The call itself, and how long you wait, is Net Etiquette. If the station still does not answer, the radio is the problem. Check these, in the order the block gives them:

CheckWhat is wrong
Line of sightTerrain is between you and them.
Sit up or stand upThe antenna is in the dirt, or you are.
FoliageBrush attenuates the signal.
Low vs highTransmit power. Low is quieter and shorter. High reaches farther and is louder to everyone else.
