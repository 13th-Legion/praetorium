13th Legion — 2nd Annual Leadership Summit

Discussion Guide & Year-Over-Year Assessment

Date: 31 May 2026 (1000–1800) · Prepared for: Command, Shop Heads, Team Leads Comparison basis: MAR 2025 survey (10 responses) vs. APR 2026 survey (12 responses)

Ground rules for this session. This is not a victory lap and it is not a gripe session. Every topic below pairs what we said last year with what actually happened. Where we delivered, we say so plainly. Where we didn't, we name it and ask why. No "we hear you." No deflection. If a thing failed, the question on the table is "why, and what changes this year so we're not reading the same complaint at the 2027 summit?" Leaders own their lanes.

Honest top-line: We had a transformational year on digital/admin infrastructure (portal, TRADOC, Recruit's Handbook, doc-signing, attendance tracking, leadership transition) and locked in some quiet structural wins (LLC/foundation status, VE team, AED). But it was a stagnant-to-failing year on physical assets (trailer), money (donations, PX), outreach (service, social media), the unfinished Militia Rifleman FM, a dropped medical program — and above all, attendance and engagement. We got dramatically better at organizing ourselves and no better at getting bodies in the field. Worth saying plainly: 41 active against 57 inactive is the number that should bother us most.

1. ATTENDANCE & ENGAGEMENT — The fight we keep losing

This is the #1 recurring failure across both years and nearly every respondent. We will spend real time here.

2025 said: - Chaos: attendance a top failure. - Delta: "Low attendance rates." - Badger: wanted "more participation from non-patched." - 2025 action item R5: "Team leads must engage and stay on top of their attendance." - 2025 action item R21: "How do we keep or get people involved and coming back."

2026 says (a year later, louder): - Command: "Attendance for FTXs has been terrible for our total membership volume." - Bravo: single biggest gripe — "Attendance." - Alpha: "Lack of attendance, lack of troop response." - Echo: "Numbers dwindled to next to nothing... had these docs up for 2 days and only heard from ONE member (Pope)." - Foxtrot: "Not enough participants. Loss of new recruits." - S3: biggest gripe — "FTX attendance, expectations, and attendance communication." - S1: "Participation."

Discussion prompts — go hard:

  1. We assigned this to team leads in 2025 ("TLs must stay on top of attendance"). Did that happen? Name names — which TLs actually ran an attendance/accountability process, and which didn't? Echo hearing from exactly one member in two days is not a member problem, it's a leadership-engagement problem. Agree or disagree?
  2. We built a portal that tracks attendance beautifully. Tracking is not improving. We can now measure the failure precisely — so what's the consequence structure? Right now there appears to be none. Should there be?
  3. Bravo, Alpha, Echo, Foxtrot — four geographic teams — all name attendance/numbers as their core problem. The 2025→2026 geographic fireteam reorg was supposed to help with local cohesion and turnout. Did it? Be specific. Alpha calls the geographic arrangement a "victory"; Echo says it grew their team; but turnout is still bad. Did we reorganize the deck chairs?
  4. Hard question for the room: Do we have a recruiting problem or a retention problem? S1 says recruiting is "knocking it out of the park." Foxtrot and Echo say they're losing new recruits. If we recruit well and still shrink, the leak is retention — and that's on the teams and the FTX experience, not the recruiters.
  5. What is the actual, enforced standard? Bravo/Delta want clarity. Define it today: how many FTXs/year is the minimum, what's the warning ladder, who enforces it. We have an Activity Policy now (6-week/2-month no-contact ladder) — is it being used, or is it a document nobody enforces?

2. THE TRAILER (and physical assets) — A year of asking, zero delivering

Requested by S1, S3, S4, Command, Chaos-adjacent in 2025. Requested AGAIN by Command, Alpha, S6, Bravo, S4, Echo, S3, Foxtrot, S1 in 2026. This is unanimous and unresolved.

Discussion prompts:

  1. This is the most-requested item two years running and it has not moved. Why? Is it money, decision paralysis, no owner, or no plan? Name the actual blocker.
  2. S1 floated concrete paths in 2026: "S4 Trailer? Chuck wagon Tatanka made? Romeo's little trailer he wants to fix up." Are any of these real options we can close on THIS summit, or are we going to ask again in 2027?
  3. Proposal for the room: Assign a single owner, a budget ceiling, and a hard deadline before we leave today. If we can't do that, we should admit out loud that we don't actually intend to buy a trailer and stop putting it on every survey.
  4. Other physical asset asks clustering in 2026: shoot house / CQB site (Command, Alpha, Bravo, Echo, S3, Delta — huge demand), thermals/NODs (S2, Foxtrot), steel targets, water buffalo, canopy spreaders. Which of these are "nice to dream big" (S2's words) and which are fundable now? Prioritize honestly.

3. COMMS — Hardware up, competence and INTERNAL comms down

The irony of the year, in the shop's own words.

2025 said: S6 — "struggle with inter-team comms, interference, many freqs no good." Multiple teams wanted a comms class. Action item R14: j-poles/antennas.

2026 says: - S6's single biggest gripe, verbatim: "NO COMMUNICATION IN THE COMMUNICATIONS SHOP!!!!!!!!!!!!" - S6: "Lack of shop personnel involvement due to shop head transition. Losing shop lead. Lack of comms training beyond mission times." - Nearly every team wants radio refreshers: S2 ("a little time to refresh at the beginning of FTX"), Alpha, Bravo, Foxtrot, Delta, S3 ("encrypted comms").

Wins to acknowledge: Comms trailer acquisition "in progress," HF radios acquired, VE certification team nearly launched, radio etiquette improved, first shop meeting + cadence set.

Discussion prompts:

  1. S6 lost its shop lead and is rebuilding under Dizz. The shop that's supposed to model communication had an internal communication collapse. What does S6 need from Command to stabilize — people, authority, a deputy?
  2. Every team independently asked for the same thing two years running: short, recurring radio refreshers at the start of each FTX. This is cheap and we still haven't institutionalized it. Decision: do we mandate a 15-min comms refresher at every FTX start, owned by S6? Yes/no, today.
  3. We bought HF but still can't hit the TSM Sunday net (Command). What's the gap — antenna, location, operator skill, licensing? Who closes it and by when?
  4. Quick-reference radio docs (HD2, mesh, comms plan) requested by S6, S2, Alpha, S1. The portal/TRADOC machine exists now — why aren't these already in it? Tasking for S6 + S3.

4. TRAINING CONTENT — Strong appetite, recurring asks, one big tension

The membership is hungry and specific about what it wants. We should feed it.

Consistent two-year demand for: - CQB / MOUT / urban (2025 S3, Delta, Command; 2026 Command, Alpha, Bravo, Echo, S3, Delta — near-universal) - Land Nav (both years, nearly everyone) - React to Contact (S3, S2, S4, Foxtrot, Bravo 2026) - Live fire / marksmanship / DMR / Sharpshooter (S3 2025; Delta, Alpha, Bravo, S4 2026) - SERE / survival / bushcraft / homesteading (Chaos 2025; Command, Alpha, S4, Bravo 2026) - Multi-tier / multi-level missions (universal, and we deliver this — keep going)

The tension worth a real fight (Delta, bluntly):

Delta 2026: "Too much classroom time given the short amount of time we have to train. Too much organization for the numbers we have." And: "S-Shops and similar roles should be exclusive to E-4 and above only." S4 echoes the time discipline problem: "We get sidetracked... a 30-minute training call turns into an hour and a half. CLEAR OBJECTIVE, AGENDA, DISCIPLINE."

Discussion prompts:

  1. Delta is saying out loud what others may feel: we've over-rotated on organization/admin/classroom and under-delivered on actual field reps. Is that fair? We're proud of the portal and the structure — has it come at the cost of trigger time and boots-in-the-dirt? Honest show of hands.
  2. CQB/shoot-house is the most-requested capability we don't have. It connects directly to Section 2 (assets). Is the answer building one, renting (Stephenville apartments / Farmers Branch warehouses were floated in 2025), or partnering? Pick a path.
  3. Delta's "S-shops should be E-4+ only" is a real governance question. Do we agree leadership/shop roles require a rank/experience floor, or do we need bodies badly enough to take whoever steps up? This cuts against S1's separate gripe (see Section 6). Debate it.
  4. Tuesday virtual training: praised by Command (quality up) AND criticized by S4/Delta (too long, unfocused). Adopt S4's proposal: every Tuesday call and every shop/team meeting gets a written objective + agenda on the calendar, hard stop on time. Agree?

5. MISSION DESIGN & RP / NARRATIVE — Build on a strength

2025 introduced two-tier missions; 2026 wants to go bigger.

  • S3: Black Swan (CBRA) storyline has been "slow to advance"; RP fluff underdeveloped.
  • S1 (swinging for the fences): "Let's go all in and make FTXs like a live-action D&D campaign. Create a story and have each FTX build off the last."
  • Common asks: longer night missions, repetition-based missions, OPFOR/force-on-force, multi-evolution short missions, rescue, LP/OP setup, FOB hardening/defense.
  • Multiple teams want inter-unit training (Echo, Command "multi-unit," S2).

Discussion prompts:

  1. Black Swan was a 2026 priority that "fizzled" (Command) and is "slow" (S3). Is Black Swan still the framework, or do we need a simpler narrative engine? S1's continuous-campaign idea is ambitious — is it realistic with our attendance reality, or does it punish the people who can't make every FTX?
  2. We do multi-tier missions well. Where's the next level — persistent OPFOR? A standing aggressor cell? Inter-unit force-on-force? Decide one signature mission concept for the year.
  3. Several teams want team-level autonomy (Chaos 2025 "missions as a team," Delta 2026 "team range day separate from unit FTX"). Do we empower teams to run their own mini-events between unit FTXs? That could also attack the attendance/cohesion problem.

6. PEOPLE, LEADERSHIP & RECOGNITION — The quiet morale issues

Smaller in word count, big in impact. Don't skip these.

The promotions/recognition tension (S1, two angles): - S1 gripe: "Ranks given for time served but not added responsibility." - S1 ask: "Recognition for people transitioning or taking on responsibilities out of necessity." - Contrast with 2025 action items R11/R12: "patched auto-promote to PV2," "point system for promotions?"

Burnout & the "go-getters" problem (recurring): - 2025 S5/3Way: "Lots of people doing solid work. There's a lot more work that can be shared among the go-getters." - 2025 S1: a core group "going way above and beyond holding the unit together." - 2026 Command: appointing qualified leaders specifically to "reduce burnout."

The volunteer-respect issue (S5 2025, sharp): - "My personal biggest gripe would be the lack of respect for my time as a volunteer" — echoed by two S5 members. Mirrored in 2026 by S4/Delta's time-discipline complaints.

Discussion prompts:

  1. Are we promoting for tenure or for contribution? S1 says we reward time-in, not responsibility. If true, that demotivates exactly the go-getters holding us together. Do we want a contribution/points-based component? (We floated it in 2025 and didn't build it.)
  2. The same small group carries the unit, every year. Burnout is a named risk two years running. Command says it appointed leaders to fix this — did it work, or did we just give the tired people titles? Check in with the actual workhorses in this room right now.
  3. Foxtrot's information-flow gripe: "Lack of communication from the top down on closing the loop on open topics... a source of info flow for members who missed the last FTX — promotions, new news, members dropped/on leave." We literally just built portal features for some of this. Is the gap the tool or the habit of using it?
  4. Recognition: cheap, high-impact, and we're not doing enough. S1 wants formal recognition for people stepping up out of necessity. Concrete proposal: a standing recognition mechanism (awards/ribbons in the portal already exist as a goal). Commit to launching it.

7. DOCUMENTS & ADMIN — From "we need everything" to "maybe less, please"

A genuine success story, with a new and healthy problem.

2025: desperate for docs — Code of Conduct, Militia Rifleman manual, ranks/structure explained, Recruit's Bible, mission planning SOP, field medic standard. Long list, little built.

2026: the tone flips — - Echo: "We have all the documents we need. Adding more could blur the lines and make it difficult for someone new." - S1: "Less documents." - S2, S4, Bravo: "command has provided everything we need; the portal has been very helpful." - Remaining real asks: Basic Militia Rifleman field manual (still not done — 2025 AND 2026), finish TRADOC docs, radio quick-refs, ERP locations/freqs, shop SOPs.

Discussion prompts:

  1. Huge credit due: we went from "document desert" to "maybe too many documents" in one year. Who built the bulk of it, and are we recognizing them? (See Section 6.)
  2. The Basic Militia Rifleman field manual is the one piece of content requested both years and still unfinished — it's meant to roll up all our TRADOC docs into a single field-manual format covering basic combat skills/tasks. It's a CO-owned item and a self-acknowledged personal failure. Assign a hard deadline today (or break it into chunks owned by the relevant shops, since the TRADOC source material largely exists) or formally kill it. The pieces are already written — this is an assembly/editing problem, not a research problem.
  3. New risk Echo raises: document sprawl creating friction for new members. Do we need an "essential 5 docs for FNGs" curated path in the portal vs. the full library? S1's "less documents" instinct agrees.
  4. Shop SOPs: Command wants all shops to draft/update SOPs this year and feed Black Swan planning. Set the deadline and the template now. This was an implied 2025 goal that didn't get standardized.

8. LOGISTICS, MONEY & THE BUSINESS SIDE — The part nobody wants to own

Quietly the area we've regressed in most.

  • Donations: 2025 wanted transparency + a visual tracker + growth. 2026: Command admits 6 months of neglect + plateau; S4 says donations declined. We went backwards.
  • PX / revenue: Talked up in 2025 (web store, tax tracking). Not stood up. Command still lists "stand up the PX and start generating revenue" in 2026.
  • LLC / nonprofit / taxes: ✅ Resolved — the LLC now files federally as a private foundation. The 2025 501c3-vs-c4 question is closed. Legal/financial footing is established; the open question is now using it (donation strategy, asset purchases), not creating it.
  • Food/meals: A real S4 win — Digger funding meals off-budget, quality up (coffee/hot chocolate), better equipment. Now needs better consumption forecasting to cut waste.

Discussion prompts:

  1. Donations regressed because no one owned the cadence. Who owns fundraising going forward, and what's the visual tracker we promised a year ago? Build it this week or admit we won't.
  2. PX: two years of talk, zero revenue. Is this real or is it a fantasy we keep listing? If real: owner, platform, first product, deadline. If not: take it off the survey.
  3. LLC/foundation status is done (private foundation). The question flips: now that we have the structure, are we leveraging it? Donation strategy, tax-deductible appeals, asset purchases through the foundation — who owns turning legal status into actual dollars?
  4. S4 self-critiqued honestly (food forecasting, team meeting leadership). Good. What does S4 need to delegate so Blackout isn't the single point of failure? (Ties to burnout, Section 6.)

9. SAFETY & SECURITY — Quietly excellent, keep it that way

The one area with consistent, unglamorous, year-over-year wins. Acknowledge it.

  • S2 2026: zero intruders, all firearms handled safely and stowed, improved gate/armory rotations, on top of local intel.
  • Foxtrot/S3 2026: "no medical emergencies," minimal injuries, safe FOB.
  • 2025 S3: "no negligent/retarded injuries, RL medical response fantastic."

Discussion prompts:

  1. We have run safe operations two years straight. That's not luck, it's S2/S5 discipline. Name it and bank it.
  2. S2's growth asks: NODs/thermals, FOB surveillance, trip-wire/alarm systems, MP-style scenario training, challenge/password set up in advance. Which are this year's priorities? The challenge/password-in-advance fix is free — just do it.
  3. Medical (S5) — the shop that went quiet, and the program that stalled. S5 did not submit a 2026 survey at all — that itself needs addressing with Hound (now S5 Lead). On the 2025 asks: we got the AED (good), did not get the CPR manikin, and every attempt to stand up a unit CPR/ECA certification program fell through — it now relies entirely on individuals knocking it out solo. The Field-Medic SOP / Medic-tab program and an official skills check-off doc still don't exist (Hound has something informal). Hard questions: Why did the unit cert program fail three+ attempts — funding, instructor, or ownership? Does medical have a real owner with time, or is it one tired person? And: should completing the medical block require a documented check-off before patching, or are we patching people on vibes? This needs a direct answer at the summit.

10. CLOSING — What we commit to before we walk out

Last year we generated a long list of action items and delivered maybe a third of them. The difference this year: every commitment gets an OWNER, a DEADLINE, and a CHECK-IN before the next summit.

Before adjourning, the room must produce a short, hard list — no more than ~7 items — each with: - One named owner (not "the shop," a person) - A deadline (a date, not "this year") - A definition of done (how we'll know at the 2027 summit whether it happened)

Candidate "must-decide-today" items, based on two years of unresolved asks:

  1. Attendance standard + enforcement — define the minimum FTXs/year, the warning ladder, and the enforcer. We have the Activity Policy written; decide today whether it's actually used. (41 active vs 57 inactive — this is the fight.)
  2. Trailer — owner, budget ceiling, deadline. (3rd year on the list if we punt. Close on S1's concrete options: S4 trailer / Tatanka chuck wagon / Romeo's trailer.)
  3. CQB/shoot-house path — build, rent, or partner. Most-requested capability we don't have. Pick one.
  4. Mandatory FTX-start comms refresher — S6 owns, 15 min, every FTX. Cheap, asked-for two years running.
  5. Donations cadence + visual tracker — one owner, restart now. We have the foundation status; now use it.
  6. Basic Militia Rifleman FM — hard deadline or break into shop-owned chunks (source TRADOC docs already exist). CO-owned.
  7. Medical program reboot — owner with actual time; decide whether a documented medical check-off gates patching; settle why the CPR-cert program keeps failing.
  8. Recognition mechanism — launch the portal awards/ribbons; reward contribution, not just tenure (S1's tenure-vs-responsibility gripe).

The test for 2027: We don't want to sit in this room next year reading the same trailer request, the same attendance complaint, and the same comms-training ask. If an item shows up a third year running, that's a leadership failure, not a membership failure. Own your lane.
